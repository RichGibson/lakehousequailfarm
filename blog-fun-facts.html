8 Surprising Facts About Quail (and Their Tiny Eggs)

By Lake House Quail Farm · 3 min read

We spend a lot of time with our quail, and they still manage to surprise us. Here are a few facts we love sharing at the farmers market.

1. They grow up in about six weeks

Coturnix quail hens can start laying eggs at just six to eight weeks old — a fraction of the time it takes most poultry to mature.

2. One hen can lay nearly an egg a day

At peak production, a healthy hen can lay close to one egg daily, adding up to roughly 200–300 eggs a year.

3. Their eggs hatch in just over two weeks

Quail eggs incubate in about 17 to 18 days, compared to 21 days for chicken eggs.

4. No two speckle patterns are alike

The mottled, speckled shell comes from pigment the hen deposits as the egg travels through her oviduct in the hours before laying. You'll never find two cartons that look quite the same.

5. Three quail eggs ≈ one chicken egg

By volume, it takes roughly three quail eggs to equal one large chicken egg — but ounce for ounce, quail eggs are especially rich in vitamins A, B1, and B2. Keep in mind both quail and chicken eggs come in a range of sizes, so this is just a general rule of thumb.

6. Quail don't need a rooster (or "cock") to lay

Just like chickens, hens will lay unfertilized eggs on their own — a male is only needed if you're hoping to hatch chicks.

7. Some people who react to chicken eggs do fine with quail eggs

We hear this anecdotally from customers fairly often — though sensitivities vary person to person, so we always suggest checking with a doctor first if you have a known egg allergy.

8. They're quiet, curious, and surprisingly quick

Anyone who's tried to catch a loose quail knows: they're fast, low to the ground, and very good at finding hiding spots. Ours have never once made it easy on us.
