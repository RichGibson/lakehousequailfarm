Quail Egg Recipes

By Lake House Quail Farm · 6 min read

Once you've got a carton of quail eggs in the fridge, it doesn't take long to start looking for excuses to use them. Here are three more of our favorites — a tangy pickle, a bite-sized party snack, and a quick weeknight fried rice.

Pickled Quail Eggs

Makes about 12 eggs · 15 minutes active, plus 3 days to pickle

  • 12 Lake House quail eggs, hard-boiled and peeled
  • 1 cup white vinegar
  • 1 cup water
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 cloves garlic, smashed
  • 1 tsp black peppercorns
  • 1 small dried chili (optional, for heat)
  • A few sprigs fresh dill (optional)
  1. Hard-boil the quail eggs: gently lower into simmering water and cook 3–3.5 minutes, then transfer to an ice bath. Peel carefully once cool.
  2. Combine vinegar, water, sugar, and salt in a small saucepan and bring to a boil, stirring until the sugar dissolves.
  3. Pack the peeled eggs into a clean pint jar with the garlic, peppercorns, chili, and dill.
  4. Pour the hot brine over the eggs, making sure they're fully submerged.
  5. Let cool to room temperature, then cap and refrigerate for at least 3 days before eating — they keep for about 3 weeks refrigerated.
A splash of beet juice in the brine turns these a beautiful magenta — purely for looks, but a fun trick for a party spread.

Deviled Quail Eggs

Makes 24 halves · 20 minutes

  • 12 Lake House quail eggs, hard-boiled and peeled
  • 3 tbsp mayonnaise
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 tsp white vinegar or pickle brine
  • Pinch of salt and pepper
  • Smoked paprika and chives, to garnish
  1. Slice each hard-boiled quail egg in half lengthwise with a sharp paring knife.
  2. Carefully scoop the yolks into a small bowl, keeping the whites intact.
  3. Mash the yolks with mayonnaise, mustard, vinegar, salt, and pepper until smooth.
  4. Spoon or pipe the filling back into the egg whites — a small piping bag or a zip-top bag with the corner snipped off makes this much easier at this scale.
  5. Dust with smoked paprika and a scatter of chopped chives just before serving.

Because quail eggs are so small, these disappear fast at a party — we usually plan on 3–4 halves per person.

Quail Egg Fried Rice

Serves 2–3 · 20 minutes

  • 8 Lake House quail eggs
  • 2 cups cooked, day-old rice
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1/2 cup diced vegetables (carrot, peas, scallion whites)
  • 1 clove garlic, minced
  • 1.5 tbsp soy sauce
  • 1 tsp sesame oil
  • Sliced scallion greens, to finish
  1. Heat 1 tbsp oil in a wok or large skillet over medium-high heat. Crack the quail eggs directly into the pan (or scramble them first, if you prefer) and cook until just set, about 1–2 minutes. Remove and set aside.
  2. Add the remaining oil to the pan, then the garlic and diced vegetables. Stir-fry for 2 minutes.
  3. Add the rice, breaking up any clumps, and stir-fry for 3–4 minutes until heated through and starting to crisp in spots.
  4. Stir in the soy sauce and sesame oil, then fold the cooked quail eggs back in.
  5. Top with sliced scallion greens and serve hot.
